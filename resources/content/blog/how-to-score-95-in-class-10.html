Scoring 95+ in Class 10 is not about studying for 12 hours every day. It is about having a clear plan, consistent practice, regular testing and disciplined revision.

Many students study hard but still lose marks because they start revision too late, don’t analyse their mistakes, neglect writing practice or spend too much time on subjects they already like. If your target is 95% or above, treat the academic year as a system:

  1. Plan
  2. Learn
  3. Practise
  4. Test
  5. Analyse
  6. Revise

What does it take to score 95+ in Class 10?

A 95% target means you cannot depend on last-minute preparation. You need to perform consistently across subjects, which requires:

  • Strong conceptual understanding and complete syllabus coverage
  • Regular homework and chapter-wise practice
  • Writing practice and previous-year questions
  • Regular tests with mistake analysis
  • Multiple revisions
  • Good time management and a proper examination strategy

Don’t wait until the Board exams to start preparing for the Board exams.

1. Start with the syllabus

Before creating your timetable, make a subject-wise list:

  1. Chapters
  2. Topics
  3. Important concepts
  4. Practice requirements

Then sort every chapter into one of three groups:

Strong

Topics you understand well.

Average

Topics requiring additional practice.

Weak

Topics requiring dedicated attention.

This immediately gives you a realistic picture of your preparation.

2. Don’t study every subject the same way

One of the biggest mistakes Class 10 students make is using the same study method for every subject.

Mathematics: understand, practise, repeat

Mathematics requires regular problem solving. Don’t simply read solved examples. Solve questions yourself.

  1. Concept
  2. Solved example
  3. Basic questions
  4. Application questions
  5. Board-level questions
  6. Previous-year questions

Never let Mathematics disappear from your timetable for several days.

Science

Science requires both conceptual understanding and answer-writing ability. For every chapter:

  1. Understand the concept

  2. Learn definitions, laws and formulae

  3. Understand diagrams and processes

  4. Solve textbook and application questions

  5. Practise previous-year questions

  6. Write answers without looking at the textbook

    This last step matters. You may know the answer but still lose marks if you can’t communicate it correctly in the exam.

Languages

Languages are often underestimated by students targeting 95+. Don’t leave them for the end. Prepare grammar, writing skills, literature, vocabulary, comprehension and answer presentation. Keep a list of your common mistakes and revise it regularly.

Social Science

For theory-heavy subjects, don’t depend on repeated reading. Use:

  1. Read
  2. Recall
  3. Write
  4. Test

After studying a chapter, close the book and ask: what can I remember without looking? Then write answers. Active recall is far more useful than rereading the same chapter.

3. Follow the 3-level preparation system

  1. Understand

    Learn the concept properly.

  2. Practise

    Solve questions without assistance.

  3. Test

    Attempt questions under examination conditions.

A chapter isn’t complete just because you’ve read it.

A chapter is complete when you can solve questions independently.

4. Create a realistic daily routine

You don’t need an unrealistic timetable. You need one you can follow consistently.

Daily blockFocus
MathematicsProblem solving
ScienceConcept + questions
Language / Social ScienceTheory + writing / recall
RevisionReview what was learned earlier
HomeworkComplete assigned work without accumulation

The exact number of hours depends on your school schedule and individual needs.

Don’t chase study hours. Chase focused study.

5. Use the 24-hour revision rule

After learning a new topic, revise it within about 24 hours, because forgetting begins quickly after learning.

  1. Day 1

    Learn.

  2. Day 2

    Quick revision.

  3. After 7 days

    Practise again.

  4. After 30 days

    Cumulative revision.

This gives the brain multiple opportunities to retrieve the information.

6. Start testing before you feel ready

A common student mindset is: “I’ll start giving tests once I finish the syllabus.” That’s backwards. Testing tells you whether your preparation is actually working.

  1. Weekly Chapter / topic test
  2. Monthly Cumulative test
  3. Later in the year Full-syllabus tests
  4. Pre-board phase Multiple full-length papers

Testing should increase as the Board exam approaches.

7. Analyse every test

This is where many students waste their effort. They get a paper back, look at the marks and move on. Instead, sort your mistakes:

Conceptual

You didn’t understand the topic.

Memory

You forgot an important fact or formula.

Calculation

Method correct, execution went wrong.

Careless

You knew the answer but made an avoidable error.

Presentation

Your answer wasn’t in the expected format.

Time management

You couldn’t complete the paper comfortably.

Then fix the specific problem.

A test is useful only if it changes what you do next.

8. Maintain a mistake notebook

Create one notebook just for mistakes. For each entry, write:

Question / topicWhere it happened
My mistakeWhat I did
Correct methodWhat I should have done
WhyWhy I made the mistake
Next timeWhat I will do differently

Revise this notebook before every major test. Your mistakes become your personalised revision material.

9. Practise previous-year papers

Previous-year papers show you question patterns, important topics, mark distribution, difficulty level, answer expectations and time management. Don’t just solve them for marks. Analyse them:

  • Which chapters appear repeatedly?
  • Where am I losing marks?
  • Can I finish within the required time?
  • Are my answers presented correctly?

10. Learn how to write board answers

Knowing the answer and scoring full marks are not always the same thing. Your answer should be:

Relevant

Answer exactly what was asked.

Structured

Use appropriate steps, headings and points.

Complete

Don’t leave out important components.

Neat

Make the answer easy to evaluate.

Supported

Use diagrams, equations, examples or keywords where appropriate.

In Science and Social Science especially, presentation can make a significant difference.

11. Master Mathematics with daily practice

If you want 95+, Mathematics cannot be a once-a-week subject. Even short, consistent practice is valuable. A useful daily set:

  • 5–10 basic questions
  • 5 application questions
  • Revision of previous mistakes

Adjust the numbers to your syllabus and level. The goal isn’t an arbitrary count.

The goal is to become comfortable solving unfamiliar questions.

12. Don’t ignore your strong subjects

“I’m already good at this subject, so I don’t need to revise it.”

Strengths fade without practice.

“I’m weak in this subject, so I’ll study only this subject.”

Other subjects slip while you fix one.

Your 95+ target depends on overall consistency. Maintain strengths while systematically improving weaknesses.

13. Build a subject-wise target

Instead of simply saying “I want 95%”, set subject-level targets. For example:

Your targets should reflect your board, subjects and current performance. The purpose is to see where you have a margin for improvement.

14. What to do three months before boards

The final three months should not be your first serious preparation phase. Your syllabus should be substantially complete by now, and the focus shifts to:

  • Revision
  • Question practice
  • Previous-year papers
  • Full-length tests
  • Weak-chapter correction
  • Time management

15. What to do one month before boards

The final month is about execution:

  1. Full-syllabus revision
  2. Timed papers
  3. Mistake analysis
  4. Weak-topic revision
  5. Repeated recall

Avoid starting large amounts of new material at the last minute unless genuinely necessary.

16. The last 7 days before an exam

Don’t try to relearn the entire subject. Focus on formulae, definitions, important concepts, diagrams, frequently tested topics, your mistake notebook, previous-year questions and writing formats.

And protect your sleep.

A tired brain doesn’t perform at its best.

17. Common mistakes students targeting 95+ make

Studying only before exams

This creates unnecessary pressure.

Fix: study consistently.

Reading without testing

You may feel prepared without actually being prepared.

Fix: test yourself regularly.

Ignoring writing practice

Knowing an answer doesn’t guarantee good presentation.

Fix: practise writing complete answers.

Leaving languages for the end

Languages can significantly affect your overall percentage.

Fix: give them regular weekly attention.

Solving questions without analysing mistakes

You keep repeating the same errors.

Fix: maintain a mistake notebook.

Following an impossible timetable

A 14-hour timetable that lasts three days isn’t a strategy.

Fix: build a sustainable routine.

A practical 95+ roadmap

  1. Foundation

    Understand the syllabus, build concepts, create study habits.

  2. Syllabus completion

    Complete chapters systematically, practise questions, take chapter tests.

  3. Consolidation

    Revise completed chapters, take cumulative tests, solve previous-year questions.

  4. Board preparation

    Full-length papers, answer-writing practice, time management, mistake correction.

  5. Final revision

    Rapid revision, weak-area correction, mock papers, confidence building.

Final 95+ checklist

Before the Board examinations, ask yourself:

  • Have I completed the entire syllabus?
  • Can I solve textbook questions independently?
  • Have I solved previous-year papers?
  • Have I taken full-length timed tests?
  • Have I analysed my mistakes?
  • Have I revised weak chapters?
  • Have I practised answer writing?
  • Can I manage the examination time comfortably?
  • Have I revised formulas, definitions and important concepts?
  • Am I maintaining consistency across all subjects?

If most answers are yes, you’re moving toward a much stronger examination position.

Final word

95+ is not achieved in the last 30 days. It is built through hundreds of small decisions throughout the academic year.

  • One chapter understood properly.
  • One set of questions solved.
  • One mistake corrected.
  • One test analysed.
  • One weak topic improved.

Repeated consistently. That’s how 95+ becomes a process, not a wish.
